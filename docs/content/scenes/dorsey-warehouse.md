# Dorsey Industrial Supply — Josh and Tyson see Frank

*Draft, 2026-10-28, from Melissa's continuity brief "Luna's Attack, the Mercer
Investigation & Frank Dorsey". Hers to rewrite. Target length 3:30–4:00.
Lines marked **(M)** are hers from the brief, kept or lightly adjusted.*

**Canon kept:** Josh and Tyson are second cousins, so Rick is not Tyson's
uncle; Frank says "Rick". Josh's mother is not established, so she is not
mentioned. Nothing about what happened at the facility is said,
nor about the journal, the Mercers by name, or who Frank calls. Nobody threatens
anybody with anything but a sentence.

---

**INT. DORSEY INDUSTRIAL SUPPLY — WAREHOUSE — NIGHT**

*8:30 PM. Half the overhead lights are on. A forklift beeps somewhere in the
back. Two EMPLOYEES are shrink-wrapping a pallet near the loading door. A
radio plays low in the office.*

*Headlights sweep across the open bay door: JOSH's white RAM 2500 pulls up
outside. The engine stops. Two doors.*

*FRANK DORSEY (58), reading glasses on, sleeves rolled, is at a workbench with
a clipboard and a coffee gone cold. He looks up at the sound of boots on
concrete. JOSH comes in first. TYSON a step behind and to the side, hands in
his jacket pockets.*

*Frank takes his glasses off. A small smile, real.*

**FRANK**
Well. Look at this. Last time the two of you walked in here together, somebody
put a forklift through my racking.

**JOSH**
That was him.

**TYSON**
I was fifteen.

**FRANK**
You were sixteen, and it was six hundred dollars, and your — *(to Josh)* — your
dad never paid me.

*Josh doesn't smile. Frank clocks it. The smile goes out of his face slowly,
like he is putting something away.*

**FRANK** *(cont'd)*
*(to the employees, without looking)*
Marco. Leave that. Go on home, both of you. I'll lock up.

*They hesitate, then go. Frank waits until the side door shuts behind them.*

**FRANK** *(cont'd)*
Rick know you boys are here? **(M)**

**TYSON**
Didn't ask him. **(M)**

**FRANK**
*(beat)*
Yeah. That's what worries me. **(M)**

*He sets the clipboard down. Squares it to the edge of the bench.*

**FRANK** *(cont'd)*
So. It's late, and you didn't call. What is it?

**JOSH**
Somebody put Luna in the hospital.

*Frank stops. This lands, and it is not performed.*

**FRANK**
Luna?

**JOSH**
Tuesday night. Downtown, walking to her truck. Somebody dragged her into an
alley and put her head into a wall.

**FRANK**
Jesus, Josh. Is she —

**JOSH**
She's okay. She's — she's got a concussion and a lot of stitches, and she
doesn't sleep. But she's okay.

**FRANK**
*(quiet)*
I'm sorry. I mean that.

*A pause. Frank looks at Tyson. Tyson's eyes haven't left him.*

**FRANK** *(cont'd)*
And you drove out here at eight-thirty at night to tell me that because —

**JOSH**
Because he gave her a message. For us. *(beat)* Said somebody's asking
questions. Said we'd know what that meant.

*Silence. The radio in the office. Frank doesn't move, but something behind his
face does: a door, closing.*

**FRANK**
And do you?

**JOSH**
I know it's about ten years ago.

**FRANK**
*(careful)*
A lot of things are about ten years ago.

**JOSH**
Not a lot of things get a woman hit in an alley.

*Frank takes his time. He picks up the cold coffee, looks at it, puts it down.*

**FRANK**
So you came here.

**JOSH**
I came here.

**FRANK**
Out of everybody in this city.

**JOSH**
Because I figured you'd know who to tell. **(M)**

*Frank lets out a breath that is almost a laugh and isn't.*

**FRANK**
You know how long I've known your family, son?

**JOSH**
Thirty years.

**FRANK**
Thirty-one in March. You learned to drive a forklift in that aisle. I drove
you to the ER the night you broke your arm off that tractor, because your father
was in Kansas City closing on something. *(beat)* And you walk into my place after
hours with *him* —

*a small tilt of the head toward Tyson —*

**FRANK** *(cont'd)*
— standing over there like that, and you tell me you figured I'd know who to
tell. You want to hear how that sounds?

**JOSH**
I haven't accused anybody of anything. **(M)**

**FRANK**
No. You've been very careful not to. *(beat)* That's your father, that is.

*That one gets in. Josh's jaw sets.*

**TYSON**
He's not here as Rick's kid, Frank.

*Frank turns and looks at Tyson properly for the first time. Tyson doesn't move,
doesn't raise his voice. He says it like a fact about the weather.*

**FRANK**
*(to Tyson, evenly)*
No. I can see that.

*He looks back at Josh. Something has changed in how he's looking at him:
appraising. The boy he watched grow up has come in alone. Rick hasn't sent him.*

**FRANK** *(cont'd)*
Your father spent a lot of years making sure some things stayed quiet. **(M,
adjusted)**

**JOSH**
You trying to tell me something? **(M)**

**FRANK**
I'm telling you to think about what you're doing. **(M)** *(beat)* You don't
know who you're talking to. You don't know who's listening. You don't even know
what the question is. You just know you're angry, and believe me, I understand
angry. But angry is how you end up standing in the wrong room saying the right
thing to the wrong man.

**JOSH**
Then tell me what the right room is.

**FRANK**
*(a long look)*
Go home, Josh.

*Josh doesn't go home. He takes a step closer, not threatening: closer, the
way you'd lean in to make sure someone hears you over machinery.*

**JOSH**
I don't care what they want. I don't care what they think they know. **(M)**

*(beat)*

**JOSH** *(cont'd)*
But they don't go near her again. **(M)**

*Frank holds his eyes. Then, slowly, he nods once. It isn't agreement. It's
receipt.*

*Josh turns to go. Tyson waits a second longer, looking at Frank, then follows.
At the bay door Josh stops without turning all the way around.*

**JOSH**
Frank?

**FRANK**
Yeah? **(M)**

**JOSH**
Make sure the right people hear me. **(M)**

*He goes. Tyson goes. The truck doors. The engine. The headlights sweep across
the bay again and are gone.*

*Frank stands at the bench a long time. The forklift battery charger ticks. He
turns off the radio in the office. The quiet is very loud.*

*He takes his phone out. Looks at it. Puts it face-down on the bench.*

*Picks it up again. Dials. Waits.*

**FRANK** *(into phone)*
You need to call me. **(M)**

*(a pause; we hear nothing of the other end)*

**FRANK** *(cont'd)*
No. Not tomorrow. Tonight. **(M)**

*He hangs up. Stands there in the half-lit warehouse with the phone still in his
hand.*

**CUT TO BLACK.**

## Notes for the shoot

- **Runtime.** About 3:45 at a natural pace with the pauses as written. The
  long holds are on Frank: after "Somebody put Luna in the hospital", after
  "ten years ago", and after Josh leaves.
- **Frank's turn.** Three beats carry him from family friend to someone who has
  to make a call: the sarcasm drops when he sees Josh's face; the door closes
  when he hears "asking questions"; he re-appraises Josh after Tyson's line.
  The last is the brief's crucial beat, where Frank realizes Josh isn't acting
  under Rick's authority.
- **Tyson** has three lines and one of them changes the scene. He never steps
  forward and never touches anything. Frank is careful around him and lets the
  audience see it.
- **Invented for texture, Melissa's to cut:** the forklift and the racking, the
  forklift aisle, the broken arm off the tractor, Kansas City, "Marco", and Luna's
  stitches and the night it happened ("Tuesday"). None of them touch the secret.
- **What Frank knows stays ambiguous.** He reacts to "ten years ago" and he
  makes the call, but he never says what about, and he never names the
  Mercers. The audience should leave unsure whether he's protecting Josh or
  warning someone.
