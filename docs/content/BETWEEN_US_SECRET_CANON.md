# BETWEEN US --- The Secret / Josh--Tyson Family History

> **Purpose:** Canon/background reference for writing *Between Us*. This
> document explains the hidden history behind Tyson's loyalty to Josh
> and Josh's father, why Tyson struggles so intensely with Luna, and how
> the secret should function dramatically.
>
> **Important:** This is writer knowledge. The audience and Luna should
> learn it gradually through breadcrumbs, not through an early
> exposition dump.

## 1. Core Present-Day Dynamic

Tyson has been Josh's best friend for most of his life. Josh and Tyson
are also distant cousins, and Tyson has effectively been treated as part
of Josh's family for decades.

Luna has known **Tyson** since they were young — she met him around age
18. She did **not** know Josh then: she met him at **28**, in Atlanta, on
business, and moved to Colorado for him. Luna and Josh were together for
**10 years** before she left. (See the correction note at the foot of this
document.)

In the present story, Tyson is in love with Luna. His resistance is
deeper than ordinary "best friend's girl" loyalty.

Tyson believes he owes Josh's family --- particularly Josh's father ---
an enormous personal debt.

To Tyson, pursuing Luna is not merely betraying Josh.

It feels like betraying the family that he believes protected his entire
future.

That belief comes from something that happened when Tyson and Josh were
about 22--23 years old.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. The Accident

Roughly **15--16 years** before the present story, when Josh and Tyson were
around 22--23, a **preventable accident** occurred on or around
property/business controlled by Josh's family.

A worker died.

The dangerous condition that contributed to the accident was **known
beforehand**.

Josh's father knew about the unsafe condition and had failed to properly
address it.

This is the central buried truth.

### Josh's position

Josh was young and beginning to take on adult responsibilities connected
to the family business/property.

Because of a decision, authorization, signature, responsibility
assignment, or other operational connection to the site, Josh came away
believing that **he may have caused the accident**.

He carried guilt for the death.

Whether Josh technically caused it is less important than the fact that
he believed he had.

### Tyson's position

Tyson was home from the military / early in his SEAL career and was
present around the aftermath.

He became entangled in what happened.

At this point Tyson was young, early in a career where scandal or legal
trouble could destroy everything he had worked for, and he already
viewed Josh's family as his own.

Josh's father stepped in.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. What Josh's Father Did

Josh's father used lawyers, company influence, authority, and control of
the situation to shape the story surrounding the accident.

He positioned himself as the man protecting **both Josh and Tyson**.

To Tyson, the message became:

**This family saved you.**

Tyson came away believing Josh's father had protected his military
career, his reputation, and possibly his entire future.

That created the debt Tyson has carried ever since.

Josh's father did not merely cover up an unfortunate accident for two
young men.

He was also protecting **himself**.

The unsafe condition had been known.

The adult with the actual power to correct it had failed to do so.

By controlling the aftermath and allowing Josh and Tyson to believe that
he had rescued them, Josh's father transformed his own culpability into
their lifelong gratitude.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. The Lie Tyson Lives Inside

Tyson's loyalty is sincere.

That's what makes this work.

He does not currently think:

> Josh's father manipulated me.

He thinks:

> That man saved my life.

Tyson therefore treats loyalty to Josh and the family almost like a
moral obligation.

He believes there are lines he simply does not have the right to cross.

Luna is the worst possible line.

So when Tyson loves Luna but refuses to act on it, his internal equation
is much bigger than:

**Josh is my best friend.**

It is closer to:

**His family gave me my future. I don't get to repay them by taking the
woman Josh loves.**

This is why Tyson can know that Luna is unhappy, know that Josh has hurt
her, know that Luna has feelings for him, and still keep retreating.

He isn't only afraid of hurting Josh.

He believes choosing himself would make him fundamentally disloyal.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Why This Matters to Tyson's Character Arc

One of Tyson's central themes is:

**Loyalty includes loyalty to yourself.**

At the beginning of the story, Tyson understands loyalty almost entirely
as sacrifice.

He protects.

He stays.

He absorbs damage.

He keeps promises.

He puts himself last.

He mistakes self-denial for integrity.

His feelings for Luna eventually force him to confront the question:

**At what point does loyalty become abandoning yourself?**

The eventual truth about the accident makes that question even more
devastating.

Tyson has structured a significant part of his adult identity around a
debt that was never what he thought it was.

The man he believed saved him was partly saving himself.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Josh's Relationship to the Secret

Josh is not simply the villain of this history.

He was young too.

He has carried his own version of the guilt.

Josh believed he might have caused the accident because of his
responsibility/decision connected to the site.

His father's intervention therefore affected Josh differently:

-   Josh carries guilt.
-   Tyson carries debt.
-   Josh's father carries the buried truth.

This history helps explain the unusually deep bond between Josh and
Tyson.

They did not simply grow up together.

They survived something together.

Neither man fully understands what actually happened.

That shared history has hardened into loyalty, guilt, obligation, and
silence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Luna's Position

Luna knew **Tyson** around this period — she had known him about five years
by then — and had **not yet met Josh**. She does not know the full truth
about any of it.

She should not understand early in the story why Tyson's loyalty to Josh
feels so extreme.

From Luna's perspective, Tyson's behavior can become maddening.

She can reasonably think:

-   Josh is an adult.
-   She is an adult.
-   Her relationship with Josh is her decision.
-   Tyson is allowed to want something for himself.
-   Tyson does not owe Josh control over the rest of his life.

She is correct --- but she doesn't know what Tyson believes he owes the
family.

This creates lines such as:

**LUNA:** "You don't owe him your entire life."

And Tyson can respond:

**TYSON:** "You don't know what I owe him."

Importantly, Luna may assume **him = Josh**.

The deeper meaning is the family and Josh's father.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. How the Audience Should Learn It

Do **not** reveal the entire accident early.

The audience should first understand that there is a history they don't
know.

The progression should feel roughly like:

### Stage 1 --- Something is off

Viewers notice that Tyson's loyalty to Josh is disproportionately
intense.

He behaves as though crossing Josh would violate something much bigger
than friendship.

### Stage 2 --- Josh's father enters the equation

Small dialogue references establish that Josh's father did something
significant for Tyson when they were young.

Example:

**JOSH:**\
"My dad treated you like one of us, Ty."

**TYSON:**\
"Yeah. I remember."

**JOSH:**\
"He would've done anything for you."

**TYSON:**\
"He did."

Cut.

The goal is for the audience to think:

**What the fuck happened between these people?**

### Stage 3 --- Tyson acknowledges the debt

Luna challenges his loyalty.

**LUNA:**\
"You don't owe him your entire life."

Tyson goes still.

**TYSON:**\
"You don't know what I owe him."

**LUNA:**\
"Then tell me."

**TYSON:**\
"I can't."

Still no explanation.

### Stage 4 --- Pieces of the old event emerge

The audience learns there was an accident.

Someone died.

Josh and Tyson were involved somehow.

Josh's father protected them.

At this point the audience can understand Tyson's behavior without yet
knowing the final truth.

### Stage 5 --- The apparent truth

The story can temporarily allow viewers to believe roughly what Tyson
believes:

Josh and Tyson were young, something terrible happened, Josh's father
stepped in, and Tyson owes him for saving his future.

### Stage 6 --- The real truth

Eventually it is revealed that Josh's father already knew about the
unsafe condition.

His intervention was not purely an act of protection.

He was controlling the story because **he had exposure of his own**.

The debt Tyson has spent years repaying was built, at least in part, on
manipulation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. What the Secret Should NOT Become

Avoid turning the history into a complicated crime thriller.

The accident exists primarily to deepen the emotional architecture of
the three men and Tyson's relationship with Luna.

The important consequences are psychological:

**Tyson:** "I owe them."

**Josh:** "I caused this."

**Josh's father:** "I protected you."

And underneath all three beliefs:

**The father knew more --- and bore more responsibility --- than either
young man understood.**

The secret should make earlier Tyson scenes hurt more in retrospect.

The audience should eventually be able to look back at every time Tyson
walked away from Luna and understand:

**He wasn't just choosing Josh. He was still paying a debt.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Writing Tyson With This Background

Even before the audience knows the secret, Tyson should behave like a
man carrying it.

When Josh or his father is invoked, Tyson may:

-   shut down rather than argue;
-   accept blame too easily;
-   become unusually rigid about loyalty;
-   avoid explaining himself to Luna;
-   use phrases like "It's not that simple";
-   react strongly when Luna says he doesn't owe Josh anything;
-   become uncomfortable when Josh's father is praised;
-   treat promises made to the family as permanent;
-   defend Josh even when he privately knows Josh is wrong;
-   choose suffering over feeling like he betrayed them.

Tyson should **not** constantly look tortured.

Most of the time this belief is simply part of his operating system.

That makes the rare moments when the subject cracks through much
stronger.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Relationship to the Luna/Tyson Slow Burn

This secret should make the slow burn more understandable without
removing the frustration.

The desired audience progression is:

**"Tyson, just fucking tell her."**

then:

**"Okay... why is he THIS loyal to Josh?"**

then:

**"Wait. What happened with Josh's dad?"**

then:

**"Oh. Now I understand why Tyson thinks he can't do this."**

and eventually:

**"Holy shit. Tyson has been destroying himself over a debt built on a
lie."**

That gives Tyson a genuine obstacle instead of making his refusal to act
feel artificially prolonged for romance pacing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. Canon Guardrails

-   Accident occurred when Josh and Tyson were approximately **22--23**.
-   It occurred roughly **15--16 years before the present story**.
-   Luna had already met **Tyson** by then, and had **not met Josh**.
-   Luna met Tyson around age **18**; she met Josh at **28**.
-   Luna and Josh had **not yet met** when the accident happened.
-   Tyson was newly in / early in his SEAL career.
-   Josh was beginning to carry adult responsibility in the family
    operation.
-   A worker died.
-   The accident was preventable.
-   Josh's father knew about the unsafe condition beforehand.
-   Josh believed he may have caused the accident.
-   Tyson became involved in the aftermath.
-   Josh's father controlled/shaped the aftermath using lawyers/company
    influence.
-   Tyson believes Josh's father protected his military career/future.
-   Tyson therefore believes he owes Josh's family an enormous debt.
-   Josh and Tyson do **not** initially understand the father's true
    culpability.
-   Luna does **not** know the full history.
-   The reveal should unfold gradually.
-   The secret's primary purpose is emotional/character-driven, not
    procedural crime plotting.
-   Present-day Luna and Josh were together for **10 years** before Luna
    left.
-   Tyson falls first in the Luna/Tyson relationship.
-   Tyson's larger thematic lesson: **loyalty includes loyalty to
    yourself.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. One-Sentence Writer's North Star

**Tyson believes Josh's family saved his future after a fatal accident
when they were young, so loving Luna feels like the ultimate betrayal
--- until he eventually discovers that the debt governing his life was
built on a version of the truth Josh's father deliberately shaped.**

### TYSON FEELS RESPONSIBLE TOO --- this is new and it is a real addition

carrying guilt for the death, and has Tyson merely *entangled in the

That is not a contradiction and it is worth stating plainly, because it
completes the document's own thesis. **Both young men took on guilt that

two people divide his culpability between them and carry it for fifteen years,
and let them. §9's summary should now read:

> **Tyson:** "I owe them --- and it was my fault."
> **Josh:** "I caused this."
> **Josh's father:** "I protected you."

**WHERE THE LADDER NOW STANDS.** Stage One is the journal

## Correction note --- 2026-09-11

Recorded rather than quietly applied, which is how corrections are handled
across this project's canon (see `LUNA_VALE_CONTEXT.md`, where the Cathy
divorce and the blonde-guy placement are both kept as records rather than
edits).

**LUNA HAD NOT MET JOSH.** The document arrived saying Luna "has known both
men since they were young" and that she "had already met them" by the time of
the accident. That is true of Tyson and cannot be true of Josh. Three places
already published on the site say she met Josh at **28, in Atlanta, on
business**: her character card, his, and `LUNA_VALE_CONTEXT.md`. The accident
is fifteen-odd years ago, when Luna was 22--23; Josh was still six years away.

It is load-bearing rather than a detail. "She left her mother, her sister and
everyone she had known since she was a child, and moved to Colorado" is the
premise of her character, and the journal entry `twenty-eight` is built
entirely on her having met him at that age.

**AND IT MAKES THE SECRET WORK HARDER, WHICH IS WHY IT IS WORTH KEEPING
STRAIGHT.** Tyson carried this for roughly six years before Luna ever met
Josh. She was already close to him through the whole of it and never knew why
he changed. Then she meets Josh, and Tyson has to watch her walk into the
family he believes owns his future --- and say nothing. The irony is sharper
than the original version and costs nothing that is already published.

There is an unwritten memory sitting in this for Luna: the year Tyson went
quiet, which she lived through at 22 or 23 and has never had an explanation
for.

**THE ARITHMETIC WAS TWO YEARS OUT.** The guardrails said the accident was
16--18 years ago AND that the men were 22--23. Tyson is 38, so 22--23 is
15--16 years ago; 16--18 years ago he was 20--22. The two guardrails
disagreed with each other. Settled at **15--16 years** and 22--23, which
keeps Tyson mid-service --- consistent with "ten years a SEAL" and with him
still serving when Luna met Josh.

Tyson carries the secret **six years** before Luna ever meets Josh, and he is
close to her for all of them. Then she meets Josh and walks into the family
Tyson believes owns his future, and he says nothing.

### The one thing that does not fit

**"Josh is already with Luna at this time."** On the numbers above, at the
accident Luna is 22, has known Tyson four years, and is six years away from
meeting Josh.

Making it true requires one of two things, and both are expensive:

- **Move the accident to ten years ago or later.** Then Josh and Tyson are 28+,
  not 22. This contradicts the age Melissa has just given.
- **Make Luna and Josh sixteen years rather than ten.** This contradicts both
  character cards, `LUNA_VALE_CONTEXT.md`, the six-months-apart premise the
  whole present-day story runs on, and the journal entry `twenty-eight`, which
  is built entirely on Luna doing this sum.

**Left as-is pending Melissa.** The accident stays at 16 years ago with Luna
not yet knowing Josh — which is also what the correction at the foot of this
document settled once already, for the same published reasons. The scene
`ty-josh-rick-accident` is staged hidden until this is decided, because a
scene that shows Luna's partner in the room dates the accident on screen.
