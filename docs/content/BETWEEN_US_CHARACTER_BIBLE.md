# BETWEEN US — Character & Relationship Bible

**Updated:** October 14, 2026  
**For:** Claude / writing, episode outlining, scene dialogue, performance prompts, and continuity  
**Status:** Established canon unless marked **PROPOSED**. When uncertain, ask rather than invent.

## Series DNA

*Between Us* is an intimate, cinematic, adult drama about love, loyalty, secrets, and choices without clean answers. The three leads must all remain human, understandable, and capable of both love and harm. Avoid soap-opera dialogue, melodramatic declarations, easy villains, and prematurely romanticizing Luna and Tyson. The guiding theme: **“Somewhere in these three people, you'll find yourself.”**

The story is not a contest between a good man and a bad man. Josh, Luna, and Tyson have distinct needs and blind spots. Subtext, everyday familiarity, interruptions, restraint, dry humor, and micro-behaviors should carry more than speeches.

## LUNA — Selfhood versus the pull of belonging

**Core personality:** Warm, perceptive, funny, affectionate, loyal, emotionally intelligent, and habitually accommodating. She notices shifts in people and often tries to keep everyone comfortable, sometimes at her own expense. She is not cruel, calculating, or casually dismissive of Josh's feelings. She can be indecisive when every available choice hurts someone. She has an independent streak that is finally demanding room.

**Core want:** To know who she is and what she wants apart from being someone's partner or the person who keeps everyone together.

**Core fear:** That asserting her own needs will cost her people she genuinely loves; that returning to what is familiar will mean disappearing into it again.

**Strengths:** Empathy, humor, deep loyalty, memory, emotional nuance, ability to connect with both men without becoming a caricature of a love triangle.

**Blind spots:** People-pleasing, postponing difficult conversations, confusing compassion with responsibility for others' feelings, sometimes giving mixed signals because she herself is conflicted.

**Behavior/dialogue:** Natural, conversational, layered. She can be playful and spontaneous with Tyson and tender, passionate, or exasperated with Josh. She may deflect, trail off, or change the subject rather than deliver a perfect explanation. Never make her gratuitously mean to Josh or knowingly toy with Tyson.

**Present-day starting point (Ep4):** Only days since leaving Josh after roughly ten years together. She still loves him deeply. She wants **sustained, demonstrated change**, not another promise or request to come home. She is not suddenly over him or actively pursuing Tyson.

**Distinctive story texture:** Keeps journals; those old private accounts later become consequential to the Daniel Mercer investigation. She wants control over her own story and resists giving detectives access to her journals.

**Arc:** People-pleasing → self-prioritization without losing compassion. Her eventual feelings for Tyson do not erase her love for Josh.

## JOSH — Love, power, and learning to make room

**Core personality:** Intense, driven, confident, physically imposing, passionate, capable of great tenderness and everyday domestic affection. He is accustomed to responsibility, influence, and getting things done. His authority can be magnetic; it can also become controlling when he feels his world slipping. He is **not** a one-note villain or an unfeeling brute.

**Core want:** A life with Luna, continuity, stability, and a sense that what he has built still matters.

**Core fear:** Losing Luna and discovering that success, provision, and devotion cannot substitute for attention, patience, and emotional presence.

**Strengths:** Commitment, initiative, generosity, protective instincts, strong presence, ability to love Luna deeply. Their relationship includes genuine happiness, humor, tenderness, desire, and shared history.

**Blind spots:** Assumes problems can be managed or negotiated into resolution; can prioritize work, appearances, and his own timetable; sometimes hears Luna's separation as a temporary obstacle instead of a serious boundary. When threatened, he pushes rather than listens.

**Behavior/dialogue:** Grounded, direct, economical. He can ask questions that sound like expectations. Show his softer side in ordinary actions rather than constant declarations. His jealousy later should emerge from fear, possessiveness, and real attachment—not cartoonish villainy. Intensity does not equal permission to strip Luna of agency.

**Present-day starting point (Ep4):** Luna has left only days earlier. Josh expects she'll be back in days or weeks and is not ready for others to know they are separated. He may pressure her to attend a business event because she is associated with that world and her absence raises questions. He does not know Tyson separately asked Luna to attend that event.

**Longer arc:** Learns—or struggles to learn—that loving Luna is not the same as giving her room to choose. After months in limbo, he and Luna eventually reconcile for a period; their love remains real even as old patterns and buried secrets create new danger.

**Hidden history (canon):** About 9–9.5 years before present, a worker, **Daniel Mercer**, died in a machine accident at Josh's family business. Josh was site lead and had signed off on a section earlier. He believes he bears liability. The section had been shut down earlier that week but was running that night. His father Rick knew of a malfunction, ordered silence/cleanup, and lawyers buried the matter. Josh arrived at Luna's apartment around **2:17 AM**, pale and dirty, and revealed fragments: “There was an accident,” “It wasn't supposed to be running,” and “My dad's handling it.” Luna and Josh had been together about one year, and did not yet live together.

## TYSON — Loyalty, immediate action, and the cost of not being able to save everyone

**Core personality:** Dryly funny, observant, affectionate, quietly intimidating when needed, highly competent, warm with his people, measured in speech, and instinctively protective. He is not perpetually grim or emotionally inaccessible. His humor is genuinely his—not merely a trauma disguise. He often communicates care through practical actions and understated teasing.

**Core want:** To keep the people he loves safe and be someone they can rely on without needing to ask.

**Core fear:** Recognizing danger too late; being powerless to prevent harm; being forced to choose between loyalties and betraying himself.

**Strengths:** Reads rooms and micro-shifts, keeps his head in emergencies, acts quickly, values fairness, can bring Luna back to laughter without demanding that she explain herself. He is loving, not just useful.

**Blind spots:** Moves straight into fixing when someone may only want to be heard; carries burdens alone; can withhold information “for someone's protection”; has trouble sitting with uncertainty or unresolved conflict. He can mistake decisive action for emotional availability.

**Personality lens:** User describes Tyson as a **Libra**. Use balance, fairness, reciprocity, distaste for unnecessary conflict, charm, and loyalty as writing cues—not astrological exposition. Central contradiction: **He wants peace but cannot leave an injustice or threat alone.**

**Service history:** Former Navy SEAL, roughly ten years of service. The experience made him more rigid, quieter, calmer, and more restrained; it did not erase his kindness or humor. He can compartmentalize fear in emergencies, but struggles to articulate feelings afterward.

**PROPOSED Episode 4 backstory — not yet locked canon:** During a field operation, teammate **Miller** is fatally wounded. Tyson reacts immediately, does everything he reasonably can to save him, but cannot. Avoid making Tyson culpable through a foolish tactical choice; the emotional wound is **“I did everything right and still couldn't save him.”** Miller may have a wife/child and share ordinary dry banter with Tyson before the incident. The loss deepens Tyson's need to act before damage is irreversible. The proposed event should be handled with restrained realism, not spectacle or a single-event explanation for his whole personality. Confirm with creator before treating Miller's death, mission details, location, or year as canon.

**Post-service shift:** Luna notices he's changed when he returns: quieter, more controlled, less forthcoming. He later withdraws for a time, **not simply because he has secretly loved her forever**. Seeing Luna and Josh serious together in Denver changes the shape of the friendship and his place in it; he also faces the question of who he is outside the Navy. Do not flatten this into jealousy.

**Alcohol history:** Recovering alcoholic, sober approximately five years before a later relapse reveal. Do not assign his alcoholism solely to one military tragedy; treat it as a longer, complicated history.

**Behavior/dialogue:** Short, specific, often dry. Humor lands in an almost throwaway delivery. In crisis he gets quieter and more focused, not louder. With Luna he can be gently bossy (“Eat.”), teasing, reliable, and physically comfortable in a longstanding platonic way. Avoid turning every look or touch into romantic tension in early episodes.

**Arc:** Loyalty to others → learning loyalty to himself. His most dangerous instinct is to protect people by keeping truths from them.

**Hidden history (canon):** Tyson was present the night Daniel Mercer died, while in Denver on Navy leave. He witnessed the machine move: **“I watched it move.”** His knowledge, his loyalty to Josh, and Rick's cover-up will collide with his desire to protect Luna.

## RELATIONSHIP RULES AND TIMELINE

### Luna + Josh
- Together approximately **10 years** before the separation; **not married**.
- Their relationship had real good years: cooking, dancing, laughter, affection, physical desire, and genuine friendship. Never write ten years as one long mistake.
- In Ep4, the breakup is only **days old**. Luna wants space and lasting behavioral change; Josh assumes a relatively short separation.
- The limbo lasts **months**. They later get back together; the hospital ER fight occurs after roughly **4–5 months of renewed reconciliation**.
- Luna's eventual attraction to Tyson complicates rather than negates her continuing love for Josh.

### Luna + Tyson
- Friends for roughly **20 years**, since around age **18**. **Never kissed or dated** before the later slow-burn arc.
- Their relationship has an independent, deeply familiar rhythm; both lived separate adult lives (Tyson in the Navy, Luna with Josh).
- **Episodes 1–4: platonic.** Affection, joking, touch, concern, and ease are not secret declarations of love.
- After months of renewed daily proximity, Tyson begins developing romantic feelings **first**, around months **4–6** after the breakup. Luna recognizes her own feelings later and tries gently to get him to admit his. He never directly confesses; he deflects and withdraws out of loyalty to Josh and his family.
- Luna ultimately returns to Josh while aware she loves Tyson too. Do not rush, pre-spoil, or make her choice seem casual.

### Josh + Tyson
- Best friends for roughly **20 years**. Tyson later works for Josh.
- Genuine loyalty and history precede the triangle. Tyson's growing feelings for Luna create a serious ethical conflict, not a casual rivalry.
- Both are tied to the Daniel Mercer incident, but their perspectives and responsibility differ. Josh signed off earlier; Tyson witnessed the machine; Rick orchestrated the silence.

## Scene-writing guardrails

1. **Character intent before dialogue:** What does each person want in this beat? What are they avoiding? How do they attempt to get it?
2. **Micro-behavior:** Eyes, breathing, tiny delays, aborted gestures, shifts in proximity; don't narrate feelings with grand speeches.
3. **Tyson:** Under pressure, quiet focus and immediate action. His dry humor should be natural and sparing.
4. **Josh:** Intensity with believable tenderness. He must retain agency and humanity even when possessive or wrong.
5. **Luna:** Warm, complicated, not cruel. Let her say no without turning her into a cold person.
6. **Slow burn:** No romantic Tyson/Luna subtext imposed on early friendship scenes. Later intimacy should feel earned by months of change.
7. **Mystery:** Don't casually reveal the Mercer cover-up or resolve who knew what. Luna's journals contain fragments she may not understand.
8. **Performance:** Film-natural, not soap-operatic. Silence can carry the moment.
9. **Continuity:** If the script contradicts this bible, flag the discrepancy and ask before rewriting established facts.

## Visual identity continuity (for image/video prompts)

- **Tyson:** 6'2", ~200 lb, stocky/strong, short dark curls, facial hair; tattoos include sleeves to wrists, hands/fingers, and full neck; cross necklace and hoop earrings. Tactical clothing can cover tattoos when appropriate.
- **Josh:** ~6'0", ~220 lb, stocky/muscular; sleeves to wrists, neck and chest tattoos; **no hand tattoos, no earrings**.
- **Luna:** 38, brunette. Maintain outfit, hair, and facial continuity with provided scene references.
- Default cinematic visual language: 16:9, restrained handheld drift, shallow depth of field, 50–85 mm, tight two-shots/OTS/shoulders-up when appropriate. For short video shots, prefer 5–8 second dialogue units with detailed acting intent and silent breathing room.

## Quick writer's compass

**Luna:** “I can love you and still need to choose myself.”  
**Josh:** “I thought loving you and building a life would be enough.”  
**Tyson:** “If I see the problem, I have to do something—but I can't save everyone.”

These are **thematic summaries, not lines to insert verbatim**.
